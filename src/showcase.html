Seven questions, seven charts

The core findings of the Canadian AI Exposure Map, one question at a time. Each card states the answer in one line and shows the chart behind it.

Substitution risk

Which Canadian jobs face the highest AI substitution risk?

Data entry (67.1% observed task coverage), customer service (70.1%), and routine finance and admin work; StatCan puts 31% of workers, about 4.2 million people, in high-exposure jobs where AI competes rather than assists.

Source: Anthropic, Massenkoff and McCrory, March 2026.
Spotlight

Are electricians at risk from AI?

Low direct risk: a 13% risk score, about 18% of tasks impacted by 2038 and mostly as augmentation, and employment projected to grow 9% as AI data centers demand more electricity. Watch conventional automation, not generative AI.

Sources: Kickresume, September 2026; MDPI, 2026; U.S. BLS, 2025-2035 projections.
Replacing vs assisting

Is AI replacing or assisting workers?

Mostly assisting: 57% of Claude work interactions augment the worker, and only about 6% of AI-adopting Canadian firms reduced employment because of AI.

Sources: Anthropic Economic Index, January 2025; StatCan CSBC, 2023 to 2026.
Geography

Where in Canada is exposure highest?

Toronto (54) and Montreal (53) top Indeed's metro index; resource metros in the Prairies and interior B.C. score lowest (44 to 45).

Index built on U.S. skill data applied to Canadian postings. Quebec's seven CMAs averaged 49.7, the highest provincial average; Alberta averaged 45.7, the lowest.
Source: Indeed Hiring Lab, October 2026.
Career ladders

Are young workers losing access to career ladders?

In coding jobs, employment of workers under 30 stagnated after November 2022 while workers aged 30 to 49 kept growing.

Lines illustrate StatCan's published finding (stagnation versus continued growth); they are not exact plotted points.
Source: Mehdi and Frenette, StatCan Economic and Social Reports, January 2026.
Training

What training actually helps?

Job-specific AI fluency learned on the job, plus judgement, relationship, and licensed skills; the Future Skills Centre's IVADO program trained more than 3,000 workers exactly this way.

Source: PwC AI Jobs Barometer, Canada cut, 2026.
Data gaps

What data is still missing?

No Canada cut of Anthropic's Economic Index exists; there is no public NOC-to-O*NET crosswalk; and the Labour Force Survey does not ask workers about AI use on the job.

  • No Canada cut of Anthropic's Economic Index has ever been published.
  • No public crosswalk links Canada's NOC occupation codes to O*NET task data.
  • The Labour Force Survey does not ask workers about AI use on the job.
  • AI-talent emigration is not measured at the occupation level.
